📖 Term Definitions expand

O.C. (On Center)The distance from the center of one stud to the center of the next.
Rough Opening (RO)The framed opening size for a door or window — not the door slab or window glass size. Check the manufacturer's instructions if unsure.
King StudFull-height stud on each side of an opening that runs from bottom plate to top plate.
Jack / Trimmer StudShorter stud beside the king stud that supports the header.
Cripple StudShort stud above a header or below a window sill that fills the layout.
HeaderHorizontal framing above a door or window opening. Size depends on load, span, and code — verify separately.
BackingExtra wood installed behind something so screws or nails have something solid to fasten to.
Drywall backingWood added so drywall edges are not floating.
Corner backing / corner postExtra wood at a wall corner so drywall and connected walls have fastening support. Examples: 3-stud corners, California corners, U-shape backing.
Butt wall / T-intersectionWhere one wall runs into the face of another wall. Also called a partition tie-in. Extra studs or ladder blocking provide fastening for the connecting wall and drywall.
Through wall / main wallThe wall that continues through while another wall connects into it.
Double wall endThe end of a wall with an extra stud for strength, fastening, drywall backing, or trim.
L-shape backingA simple connection where one perpendicular stud helps one wall connect to another.
U-shape backingExtra studs forming a U so two perpendicular walls have backing on multiple drywall faces.
Ladder blockingHorizontal blocks between studs to create fastening support for a wall intersection or drywall edge.
Fire blockingMaterial installed to slow fire, smoke, and air movement inside cavities. A code/safety item, not just fastening backing.
Fixture backingBacking installed for cabinets, handrails, grab bars, TV mounts, shelving, or other mounted items.
SheathingPanel material such as OSB or plywood installed over framing. Different from studs, plates, or blocking.
Load-bearing wallA wall that carries vertical loads from above (roof, floor). Usually gets a double top plate. Headers are sized for load. Verify with drawings or a qualified person.
Non-load-bearing wallA wall that does not carry structural load from above. Often a single top plate is used, but check requirements.
